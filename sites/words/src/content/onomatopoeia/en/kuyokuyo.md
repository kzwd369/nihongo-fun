## The Sound of Not Letting Go

クヨクヨ (kuyokuyo) describes the mental loop of dwelling on something — replaying a mistake, worrying about a decision, or being unable to shake off regret. It's a **gijougo** (擬情語), an emotion word, and it captures that particular kind of low-energy sadness where your mind keeps circling back to the same painful thought.

## When クヨクヨ Comes Up

You'll often hear クヨクヨ in the phrase 「クヨクヨするな」 (kuyokuyo suru na, "stop brooding" or "don't dwell on it") — the kind of encouragement friends give when someone can't move past a setback. It's also commonly paired with 悩む (nayamu, to worry): 「クヨクヨ悩む」 means to worry in a way that goes nowhere, just circling the same ground. The word implies that the worrying isn't productive — it's the kind that holds you in place rather than leading to a solution.

## Fun Fact

クヨクヨ is often contrasted with サッパリ (sappari, refreshed and clean-slated) in self-help contexts and advice columns. The idea is to move from a クヨクヨ state to a サッパリ one — from endlessly replaying the past to letting it go and feeling lighter. This pairing reflects a common theme in Japanese emotional vocabulary: many onomatopoeia come with a natural opposite.
