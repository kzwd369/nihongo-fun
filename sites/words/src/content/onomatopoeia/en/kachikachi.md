## Hard as a Rock, Stiff as a Board

かちかち (kachikachi) is the word for something that has gone completely hard — no flex, no softness, no give. It's a **gitaigo** (擬態語) that describes a state of rigid solidity, covering everything from a frozen rice ball to a person who is so tense their whole body has locked up. The repeated かち-かち rhythm reinforces the sense of something unyielding and resistant.

## Physical Hardness and Nervous Tension

The most literal use of かちかち is for physical hardness: ice, frozen ground, stale bread, dried-out clay. 「かちかちに凍る」 (kachikachi ni kooru, "frozen rock-solid") is a phrase you'll encounter every winter in Japan. But the word extends naturally into the emotional realm too. 「かちかちに緊張する」 (kachikachi ni kinchou suru, "to stiffen up with nerves") describes a speaker whose body tenses before a presentation, or a student who freezes during an oral exam. The underlying image is the same: something that should be flexible has become rigid.

## Fun Fact

Japanese speakers familiar with folklore may associate かちかち with the famous tale "Kachi-Kachi Yama" (かちかち山), in which a tanuki (raccoon dog) is tricked by a rabbit who sets fire to a bundle of sticks on the tanuki's back — the かちかち in the title is said to represent the clicking sound of a flint being struck. The story is well-known enough that many Japanese people may hear a faint echo of it whenever the word comes up, even in everyday conversation about something frozen solid.
