## The Sound of Being Completely Soaked

ビショビショ (bishobisho) is the word for being thoroughly, unmistakably drenched. It's a **gitaigo** (擬態語) — describing a state rather than a sound — and it paints a vivid picture: clothes clinging to skin, hair dripping, water pooling in your shoes. There's no ambiguity with ビショビショ; it means soaked through and through.

## Degrees of Wetness

Japanese has a useful scale for wetness. しっとり (shittori) is lightly moist or dewy. じっとり (jittori) is unpleasantly damp, like sticky humidity. ビショビショ is the extreme end — completely drenched with no dry spot left. The most common pattern is 「ビショビショになる」 (bishobisho ni naru, "to get soaking wet"), and it applies to people, clothes, floors, or anything else that can be thoroughly soaked.

## Fun Fact

ビショビショ is closely related to びしょ濡れ (bisho nure, "soaking wet"), which uses the same びしょ root paired with 濡れ (nure, "wet"). The exact origin of びしょ is uncertain — like many onomatopoeia, it likely arose from the feel of the sound itself rather than from a specific word. Either way, ビショビショ is one of those satisfying cases where the sound just feels wet.
