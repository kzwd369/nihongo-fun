## The Sound of Cutting It Close

ギリギリ (girigiri) is the word for that breathless, just-barely-made-it feeling. It's a **gitaigo** (擬態語) — describing a state rather than a sound — and it captures the sensation of being right at the edge of a limit. Whether it's catching a train as the doors close or scraping through an exam with the minimum passing score, ギリギリ is about surviving by the thinnest possible margin.

## Deadlines, Limits, and Daily Life

ギリギリ appears constantly in everyday Japanese. 「ギリギリセーフ」 (girigiri safe) means you just barely made it — safe by a hair. 「ギリギリアウト」 (girigiri auto — アウト being the Japanese rendering of English "out") is the painful opposite: you missed it by just a fraction. You'll also hear 「ギリギリまで」 (girigiri made, "right up until the limit") to describe people who procrastinate or push things to the absolute last moment.

## Fun Fact

The word ギリギリ may come from the sound of grinding or twisting something tight — like turning a screw to its limit (ギリギリと締める). That physical sense of being wound to the breaking point carried over into the modern meaning of being pushed right to the edge. Some linguists also connect it to the idea of shaving something down to nothing, leaving zero room to spare.
