## The Word for a Perfect Match

ぴったり (pittari) is one of those Japanese words that feels satisfying just to say. It's a **gitaigo** (擬態語) — describing a state rather than a sound — and it captures the sensation of something fitting exactly right, with zero gap or margin of error. A lid that clicks perfectly onto a container, a pair of shoes that feels tailor-made, a train pulling in at the exact scheduled minute: all ぴったり.

## Sizes, Timing, and Predictions

ぴったり appears in three main contexts. First, **physical fit**: 「ぴったりのサイズ」 (pittari no saizu, "the perfect size") is something you'll hear in clothing shops constantly. Second, **timing**: 「時間ぴったり」 (jikan pittari, "right on time") describes punctuality with zero deviation — a trait the Japanese rail system is famous for. Third, **accuracy**: 「ぴったり当たる」 (pittari ataru, "to hit the mark exactly") works for predictions, guesses, and quiz answers. You may also hear 「ぴったり合う」 (pittari au, "to match perfectly"), which covers everything from puzzle pieces to personality compatibility.

## Fun Fact

The small っ (double consonant) in ぴったり is doing a lot of work. That sharp stop before the た gives the word a sense of something snapping cleanly into place — a crisp, decisive click. Compared to a hypothetical ぴたり (which does exist as a more literary variant), the doubled consonant in ぴったり adds a stronger feeling of completeness. Some language researchers suggest this pattern — where geminate consonants intensify the sensation — runs through much of Japanese sound symbolism.
